# CSC586C Related Work Assignment

**Deliverable:** A spreadsheet summarizing scholarly literature, identifying connections between studies, and reflecting on its relevance to your research (one paper per row). Aim for **around 20 papers in total, including the core course readings and several additional papers you identify independently**.

## Purpose

This assignment intends to give you a overview of recent research in Human-AI Collaboratio and help you develop the research foundation for your empirical case study in CSC586C. Your reading should inform your investigation of responsible human–AI collaboration in software development.

For each paper, explain both what it contributes and how you might use that contribution. Consider how it could shape your research questions, guide your methods, help you interpret observations, or challenge your assumptions. You must also identify relationships between papers so that your spreadsheet develops a connected understanding of the literature.

## Selecting Your Readings

Include the core readings provided through the course schedule and find several additional research papers relevant to your emerging research direction. The suggested total of around 20 papers **includes the core readings**.

Additional papers may come from software engineering or other relevant fields, provided you explain their connection to your study. Include work that challenges or complicates your thinking, as well as work that supports it.

## What to Record About Each Paper

Provide a full reference, including authors, year, title, and publication venue, together with a link or DOI. Paste the **original article abstract verbatim** into the abstract column, clearly identified as the authors’ text.

Summarize the key research questions, methods, and findings in your own words:

- **Research questions or objectives:** Identify the questions the paper addresses. If it does not state formal research questions, summarize its stated objectives.
- **Methods:** Describe the research design, study context, participants or data sources, and analysis approach where relevant. For conceptual or review papers, describe the approach used.
- **Findings or arguments:** Summarize the main findings or central arguments. Distinguish what the paper establishes from what the authors suggest or propose.

## Reflecting on What You Will Borrow

The reflection columns should explain how each paper informs your own research.

- **Relevance to my research:** Which aspect of your case study does the paper help you investigate? Connect it to a specific question, observation, problem, or research decision.
- **Methods I could borrow or adapt:** Which data collection techniques, measures, research designs, or analysis approaches could you use? Explain what you would adopt, what you would change, and why.
- **Concepts and terminology I could use:** Which concepts or definitions could help you describe and analyze what you observe? Explain how you would use them.
- **Bigger ideas and assumptions:** Which theories, frameworks, broader arguments, or assumptions influence your thinking? Explain whether you would adopt, adapt, question, or test them.
- **Critical assessment and limits of application:** What makes the paper’s contribution useful or less applicable to your study? Consider its evidence, context, limitations, and assumptions.

Be specific. **“I could use their interview method” is too general.** Explain which aspect of the method you would use, what it would help you investigate, and how you would adapt it to your case study. 



A paper does not need to contribute equally to every borrowing category. Where a category does not apply, simply put NA rather than forcing a connection. Every paper should nevertheless include substantive reflection on its overall relevance and limitations. Thus, if you have put NA many times, it is possible you have not chosen a paper that will adequately support your research endeavour. 

## Identifying Relationships Between Papers

For each paper, identify and explain its relationship to one or more other papers **included in your table**. Identify those papers by DOI or author–year citation and explain the nature of the connection.

For example, a paper might:

- support, extend, or contradict another paper’s findings;
- investigate a similar question using a different method or context;
- use the same concept differently or rely on a different underlying assumption;
- provide a theoretical explanation for another paper’s empirical findings;
- reveal a limitation or unresolved question when considered alongside another paper.

An illustrative relationship statement might read:

> Compared with [Author, Year], this paper uses interviews rather than repository data to examine developer trust. It adds evidence about how developers explain their decisions, which could inform the interview component of my case study.

Simply stating that two papers are “both about AI” is not sufficient. Explain what their relationship helps you understand and, where relevant, what it means for your research. Revisit these connections as you add papers.

## Expectations

Read beyond the abstract so you can accurately explain each paper’s contribution. Apart from the copied abstract and any clearly identified quotations, write your entries in your own words.

Use concise paragraphs or bullet points within spreadsheet cells. The emphasis is on thoughtful selection, accurate understanding, meaningful connections between papers, and specific reflection on how the literature informs your research.


## In-Class Presentation of Related Work

After submitting your related work spreadsheet, you will present a summary of your review in class. Your presentation should explain what the literature reveals about your research topic and how it is shaping your case study in CSC586C.

Organize your presentation around the major themes, questions, and debates emerging across the papers. Draw on both the core readings and the additional papers you identified, emphasizing those most relevant to your research direction.

Your presentation should address:

- **Research focus:** Introduce the topic or question guiding your review and its relevance to responsible human–AI collaboration.
- **Key themes and findings:** Explain the main ideas and findings emerging from the literature, including important concepts, theoretical perspectives, or methodological approaches.
- **Relationships between papers:** Discuss where studies support, extend, or contradict one another. Consider whether differences in methods, contexts, definitions, or assumptions help explain their conclusions.
- **Implications for your research:** Explain what you intend to borrow or adapt from the literature, such as methods, terminology, frameworks, or broader ideas. Justify how these contributions will inform your case study.
- **Gaps and emerging questions:** Identify what remains unresolved and explain how your review has helped you refine the questions you want to investigate.

Clearly cite the papers you discuss. Your presentation should demonstrate your ability to synthesize the literature, critically assess its relevance, and use it to make informed research decisions.

Be prepared to discuss your interpretations and respond to questions from the instructor and your peers. Use this discussion to refine your research direction and identify areas where further reading is needed.
