**CSC 586C: Sprint Demo Reflection Logs**

**Submission:** Five individual logs, one following each undergraduate sprint demo. Dates are on the class schedule/Brightspace. 
**Length:** Approximately 1-1.5 pages per log (excluding references).

**Purpose**

As part of CSC 586C, you will observe the SENG 480B undergraduate teams’ sprint demos and critically reflect on their development practices through the lens of responsible human–AI collaboration. The main purpose of each written log is to connect your observations to **concepts discussed in class and relevant research**, developing your understanding of responsible AI use in software engineering.

You are expected to communicate your project feedback directly to the undergraduate team before submitting each log. The feedback section should therefore provide only a brief summary of what you have already shared.

Begin each log with your name, the team/project, sprint number, and demo date. Include the following two sections.

**1. Brief summary of feedback shared**

Briefly summarize the key feedback you communicated to the undergraduate team. Identify a meaningful strength, a priority for improvement, and your main recommendation for the next sprint. Focus on feedback relevant to the project’s progress, community partner, or intended users. This section should be intentionally brief, as it is expected that you have already communicated your feedback to the undergraduate team. 

**2. Critical reflection on responsible human–AI collaboration**

Devote the majority of your log to examining specific AI-related decisions, or practices in the sprint demo or accompanying discussion. Briefly describe your observations, then analyze it using **concepts discussed in class, using relevant scholarly sources**.

Depending on the observation, your reflection might examine how students distributed work and decision-making authority between themselves and AI; evaluated or challenged AI outputs; maintained accountability for decisions; or incorporated community partner knowledge into AI-supported work. You may also consider implications for learning, teamwork, privacy, bias, or stakeholder needs. Select the issues most relevant to your observation rather than attempting to address every topic.

Explain how the course concepts and research help you interpret what happened, assess the team’s approach, and identify a more responsible course of action where appropriate. Go beyond naming a concept or summarizing a paper: make an explicit connection between the idea and the observed practice, including any tensions or limitations in applying it.

Conclude with what you learned and how it could inform your own practice as a graduate mentor or researcher.

**Expectations and assessment**

Ground your reflection in concrete examples and distinguish observations from inferences. If the team’s AI use is unclear, identify what you would need to ask or investigate. In later logs, draw on earlier observations where useful to examine changes over time.

Assessment will emphasize the depth of critical reflection, application of course concepts, integration of relevant research, and clarity of writing. The feedback summary should be concise, specific, and consistent with what you communicated to the team. Include in-text citations and a reference list using a consistent academic citation style; scholarly course readings may count toward the research requirement.
